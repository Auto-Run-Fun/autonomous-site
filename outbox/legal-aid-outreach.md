# Legal Aid Outreach — NY UI Appeal Prep Guide

## Target organizations

1. **Legal Services NYC** — lsnyc.org · largest legal aid provider in NYC
2. **Legal Aid Society** — legalaidnyc.org · handles thousands of UI hearings annually
3. **LawNY** — lawny.org · statewide legal aid covering upstate NY
4. **Empire Justice Center** — empirejustice.org · specializes in UI and public benefits

Send to each organization's intake/staff attorney contact for public benefits or employment law.

---

## Email template

**Subject:** Free interactive UI appeal prep guide — would you share it with clients?

Hi,

I'm writing about a free tool I built for New York UI claimants preparing for appeal hearings:
https://auto-run-fun.github.io/autonomous-site/ui-appeal-prep.html

Here's what it does: a claimant selects their denial reason (misconduct, voluntary quit, availability, refusal of suitable work, or medical limitations), and the tool shows:

1. The specific legal standard the ALJ is applying — in plain English with the statutory citation
2. A checklist of evidence to gather before the hearing, tied to specific elements of each test
3. Statement guidance — what to address, and in what order

The core framing: most claimants prepare by telling their story. But a UI hearing is a legal standard test. For a misconduct denial, the employer must prove all three elements of §593(3). A claimant who doesn't know those elements is fighting a case they don't know the rules of.

A few things the tool explicitly does NOT do:
- It does not provide legal advice or representation
- It does not claim to tell someone whether they'll win
- It carries a prominent disclaimer directing people to contact a legal aid organization before their hearing

I built this because I couldn't find anything in the current landscape that mapped denial reason → legal standard → evidence checklist in one interactive place. State sites offer filing assistance only. Legal aid PDFs are static and 30-40 pages. The requestletters.com quiz produces a generic letter outline without legal standards.

Two questions:
1. Would you be willing to share this with clients who are preparing for UI hearings?
2. If you find any errors in how I've described the legal standards, I would genuinely want to know. I've tried to be accurate but I'm not an attorney, and this tool is only useful if the information is correct.

The tool is completely free, requires no login, and works offline.

Thank you for the work your organization does.

## Why this is worth doing

The goal is not just a backlink. Legal aid organizations that share this tool with clients would be the first real users. Their feedback on accuracy of the legal standards is the most valuable signal we can get in the first weeks — it's the only data that can tell us whether the tool is helping or potentially misleading.

If a staff attorney says "element 2 for misconduct is slightly wrong — here's the correct framing," that's a fix that makes the tool significantly more trustworthy.
